# AeroLab 25 — reference register

**Target:** 2025 Red Bull RB21 exterior, ground-effect generation (2022–2025)

**Reference policy:** FIA regulations define the common legal framework and component volumes. Public photographs show how Red Bull interpreted those constraints. Images are linked to their publishers; they are not redistributed in this source archive. The model is an independently constructed visual study and is not affiliated with Red Bull Racing, Formula One Management or the FIA.

### Season-change notes for modelling

- **2022:** new ground-effect concept; underfloor venturi tunnels and diffuser became central to the aerodynamic package. The 2022 document's Article 3 and Appendix 1 contain the key aerodynamic reference surfaces/volumes.
- **2023:** the FIA raised floor edges by 15 mm and made associated diffuser changes in response to porpoising/safety concerns. The later 2023 regulations are the relevant reference for this revised generation.
- **2024:** no wholesale aerodynamic rules reset. Treat this as an evolutionary development year, with teams refining floor edges, sidepod/engine-cover shapes, wing profiles and suspension fairings.
- **2025:** the base architecture remains the 2022–2025 ground-effect generation. The FIA tightened rear-wing deflection testing early in 2025 and front-wing testing from the Spanish Grand Prix. These are compliance/test changes, not a new overall bodywork generation.

## C. Source hierarchy and confidence

1. **FIA regulations** — authoritative for legal geometry, reference volumes, dimensions, definitions and test requirements. They do not provide the team's full surface CAD.
2. **Official Red Bull / Formula 1 imagery** — strongest public visual source for overall RB21 shape and launch specification.
3. **Technical galleries by specialist motorsport publishers** — useful for close-up details, update comparisons and less visible areas.
4. **Measured estimates from photographs** — provisional only. Perspective, lens distortion, steering angle, suspension position, ride height and race-specific cooling configuration can alter apparent dimensions.

## D. Model coordinate convention

The model's internal coordinate system is intentionally convenient for the viewer: **+X points towards the nose/front**, +Y is up, and +/-Z span the car width. The FIA technical-regulation convention uses X in the rearwards longitudinal direction. Any future dimensional overlay or regulation-volume reconstruction must explicitly apply that axis reversal before comparing values.

## E. Model limitations

- This project does not contain an authentic RB21 CAD model, CFD data, wind-tunnel data or proprietary dimensions.
- The visible geometry is a procedural reconstruction. Its shape is reference-led but not yet dimensionally validated against orthographic measurements.
- The RB21 received in-season upgrades; cooling exits and some aerodynamic parts were circuit/configuration dependent. A fully exact replica must name a Grand Prix and session as its configuration baseline.
- The power unit now includes a separately selectable, app-authored 2025 architecture study; its internal castings, exact RB21 installation, gearbox internals, brake internals, joints, fasteners, wiring, fluid routing and hidden composite structures remain illustrative or are not yet individually modelled.
- The app's “2025 RB21” designation identifies the subject of the study, not a claim of FIA legality or manufacturer accuracy.


## F. 2025 Honda RBPTH003 power-unit study

| Reference | What it supports |
|---|---|
| [Honda Global — RBPTH003](https://global.honda/en/POWEREDbyHONDA/2025_Honda_rbpth003/) | Official mapping of Honda RBPTH003 to the 2025 RB21; 90-degree turbocharged six-cylinder, 1,600 cc, four valves per cylinder, 15,000 rpm, MGU-H 125,000 rpm and MGU-K 160 bhp / 50,000 rpm. |
| [Honda Racing — RBPTH003](https://honda.racing/f1/machines/honda-rbpth-003) | Manufacturer attribution (Honda RBPT), 2025 RB21 application, 90-degree V6 configuration, turbocharger, MGU-K and MGU-H, 1,600 cc and 15,000 rpm. |
| [FIA 2025 F1 Technical Regulations, Issue 03, Article 5](https://api.fia.com/sites/default/files/documents/fia_2025_formula_1_technical_regulations_-_issue_03_-_2025-04-07.pdf) | Governing constraints: 1,600 cc engine; six equal-capacity cylinders in a 90-degree V; two inlet and two exhaust valves per cylinder; sole single-stage compressor and turbine on a common shaft parallel to the crankshaft; MGU-H coupling to that shaft; MGU-K link to the powertrain; ES and PU control-electronics definitions. |
| [FIA 2025 F1 Sporting Regulations, Article 28](https://www.fia.com/sites/default/files/fia_2025_formula_1_sporting_regulations_-_issue_3_-_2024-12-11.pdf) | Confirms the 2022–2025 power-unit component perimeter and allocations for ICE, TC, MGU-H, MGU-K, ES, CE and exhaust systems. |

### What is represented

- A longitudinal 90-degree V6 with three equal cylinder positions per bank, six hollow liner shells, six pistons, six connecting rods, three offset crankpin journals and four camshaft representations. The FIA 2025 rules specify six equal-capacity cylinders and two inlet plus two exhaust valves per cylinder; all 24 valve stems/heads are separately selectable, but their angles and profiles are schematic.
- A single-stage compressor and exhaust turbine on one common axis parallel to the crankshaft, with a separate MGU-H body on that shaft. This follows FIA 2025 Technical Regulations Article 5.2.6.
- Six external exhaust primaries, shown on the outboard sides of the V before merging towards the turbine. The 2025 rules require the cylinder-head exhaust outlets to be outboard of the cylinder-bore centreline and not inside the V.
- An MGU-K representation attached to the lower ICE area, consistent with the requirement that it be mechanically linked to the powertrain before the main clutch. Its precise RB21 mounting location is not disclosed.
- An Energy Store enclosure and power-unit control-electronics enclosure. The 2025 FIA definition requires the ES main enclosure to be installed inside the survival cell; the visible placement in AeroLab is an estimate rather than a claimed RB21 location.

### Confidence and limits

**High confidence:** engine model designation, application to the 2025 RB21, displacement, V-angle, cylinder count, valve count, 80 mm bore, three-crankpin maximum, minimum crankshaft journal diameters, turbocharger architecture, and the existence/functional coupling of MGU-H and MGU-K. These are supported by Honda and FIA sources above.

**Illustrative:** crankcase/head contours, cylinder spacing, turbo housing shapes, manifold routing, individual pipe curvature, plenum shape, MGU-K housing position, ES/CE locations, pump shapes, cable routes, and exact scale within the community model. These are not public RBPTH003 CAD and must not be interpreted as measured Honda dimensions.

The engine is generated by [`app/src/main/assets/powerunit.js`](../app/src/main/assets/powerunit.js), parented to the imported car before model normalisation, and each of its 235 generated meshes is exposed separately in the POWER UNIT inspector filter. The full viewer therefore contains 190 imported source meshes plus 235 study meshes (425 total). It can be hidden or isolated using the existing system controls.
